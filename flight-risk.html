Model and assumptions

This is an illustrative conditional scenario model. It compares airborne infection risk under a selected flight scenario with and without an illustrative GUV intervention. It is not a forecast for any specific flight, traveler, airline, or year.

Interactive inputs

Quanta emission rate (q): 0.035–3.1 quanta/hour. The four labeled points are resting, oral-breathing median marker values from Mikszewski et al. (2021): seasonal influenza 0.035, rhinovirus 0.21, SARS-CoV-2 0.55, and measles 3.1 quanta/hour. Mikszewski et al., Geoscience Frontiers. The slider may take any value in this range; the four values are marker values and snap points only.

Infectious passengers (I): 1–10. This is a user-selected conditional scenario, not an estimate of infection prevalence on U.S. flights. The susceptible passenger count updates automatically as 96 − I.

Flight duration (Δt): 1–11 hours. Repeated flights (m): 0–10 one-way flights/year. Both are user-selected scenario inputs.

Model equations

Rebreathed-air fraction [1]: f = (CO₂ cabin − CO₂ background) / Cₐ Single-flight infection probability for one susceptible passenger [1,2]: P = 1 − exp[−(I × q / n) × f × Δt] Expected secondary infections on one flight [1]: E = (n − I) × P Cumulative risk for one susceptible traveler over m comparable, independent flights (derived in this model): P_cum = 1 − (1 − P)^m Illustrative GUV scenario [3]: q_GUV = q × (1 − 0.75) Illustrative U.S. domestic annual economic burden [4]: B = E × $138 × 8,777,000

Heatmap color scale. Both panels use the same cumulative infection-risk scale, from 0% to the highest-risk combination available in this model. With the current parameter bounds, that upper limit is 57.01%, which occurs at q = 3.1 quanta/hour, 10 infectious passengers, 11 hours, and 10 flights/year. To make differences at lower risk levels easier to see, the heatmap uses a square-root color mapping. This changes only the visual shading, not the calculated risk values.

Interpretation. The annual economic value is a scale-up of the currently selected flight scenario across 8.777 million comparable departures. It combines modeled secondary infections with an approximate generic medical + absenteeism-related cost per respiratory illness episode. It is not a forecast of actual annual U.S. infections or cash savings, and it does not include willingness to pay, pain and suffering, QALYs, VSL, or long-term sequelae. The 75% GUV value should be read strictly as an illustrative upper-bound scenario rather than a validated aircraft-specific performance estimate.
